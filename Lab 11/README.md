This project applies unsupervised machine learning to segment credit card customers based on their financial behavior. Using the CC_GENERAL dataset—which includes metrics like account balances, purchase volumes, and cash advance frequencies—the goal is to group similar customers together to help the business understand its user base and design highly targeted marketing strategies.

The data science workflow involves handling missing values, performing exploratory data analysis, and standardizing the features to ensure the distance-based clustering algorithm performs accurately. To find the optimal number of segments, both the Elbow Method and Silhouette Score were evaluated, leading to the selection of K=3. The K-Means algorithm was then applied to assign customers to clusters, and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was used to compress and clearly visualize these groupings in a 2D space.

Ultimately, the model identified three distinct customer profiles: High Spenders who make frequent purchases and pay off balances consistently, Conservative Users who are low-volume cardholders, and Cash Advance Reliant customers who carry high balances but make few purchases. Understanding these segments allows the business to tailor its approach, such as offering premium rewards to high spenders while designing sensible debt-management products for those relying heavily on cash advances.
